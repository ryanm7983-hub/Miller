# The Black Pine — story

**Spoilers throughout.**

---

## Premise

The Black Pine Basin was logged out by Vantry Timber in the 1950s. The company
town is gone. In 1974 a Forestry Service acoustics programme installed eleven
passive listening stations across the valley floor; they were decommissioned in
1989 and the relay was cut.

Last month they started transmitting again.

You are **R. Aldaugh**, a contract field acoustician. Work Order 41-C: attend
site, determine the source, restore or terminate as appropriate.

## What is actually happening

The black pine of this basin lays down resin in a fine annual lamina, and the
soil is unusually rich in a ferrous silicate. Where the two meet, the lamina
takes an impression of pressure — and sound is pressure. **The rings are a
recording.** Ivo Marsh proved it in 1976 by recovering forty-one seconds of
Norwegian speech from a stump felled in 1953.

The basin does not hold people. It holds the *shape* people leave, and when it
plays that shape back it is filled in by whoever is listening. Halden Rill, the
last ranger, worked this out in November 1989:

> The first month I heard my wife. Not her voice — her step, the particular drag
> of her left boot on the porch board. It took me four nights to understand that
> I was the one putting her in it.

That is the horror, and it is the mechanic. Every recording the player plays
back teaches the basin more of their voice.

## The two arguments

The story is a disagreement between two people who are both already gone.

**Ivo Marsh** believes the basin is a medium and a medium has no appetite. There
are people in this valley who were never listened to; he wants to restore the
relay, make the passive net *active*, and let them be heard — by everyone.

**Halden Rill** believes Marsh is right about the mechanism and wrong about
everything that follows from it. He spent a winter alone taking the posts down,
got nine of eleven, and stopped speaking aloud in October because everything he
had said was still out there and had been practising.

The player's documents arrive interleaved, so both positions land before either
resolves.

## The eleven

In March 1953 the night shift at Vantry No. 3 was lost — eleven men, no remains
recovered. The company posted a notice, paid the wages, and declined further
discussion of noises at the shaft head.

The men buried eleven empty graves and then, one starting and the rest joining,
said the eleven names aloud three times, "because it was felt that saying them
once was not enough". Somebody later went along the row with a chisel and took
the names back off the stones.

The Standing Ring is what they built afterwards: eleven posts, and a bark
bundle wired to one of them.

> we set the stones over nothing so we brought them a something
> we gave it the names to hold
> eleven names is a weight
> a weight will keep a thing in one place
> **do not take the names off it**

The twelfth stone at the end of the cemetery row is newer, and blank, and the
ground in front of it has been turned recently. On the shift board in the mine,
the rubbed-out bottom row has a twelfth name written into the smear in a
different hand, much later. It is yours.

## The three reels

| Reel | Where | What is on it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MARROW 04 | first playback | Marsh and Rill, working, laughing. The last four seconds repeat three times at slightly different pitch. |
| SORREL 11 | second playback | A man's voice saying "Halden Rill" for nine minutes. The pronunciation improves. |
| unmarked | third playback | No room tone at all — not silent, *empty*. At 4:11, in the player's own voice, a question they have not asked yet. |

## Structure

Four chapters, driven by state rather than triggers, so the player can do
things out of order and the game keeps up.

1. **Arrival** — reach Vantry Ranger Station.
2. **Network** — bring three listening posts online.
3. **Archive** — find what the network has been recording.
4. **Decision** — the relay, the standing ring, or the fire road.

## Endings

| Ending | How | What it costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **TRANSMIT** | Restore the relay and broadcast the basin. | Marsh's answer. Several thousand strangers now have a hole shaped like a person and will fill it in with whatever is missing from them. It has been handed on. |
| **SILENCE** | Take the network down and burn the resin archive. | Rill's answer. It works. Halfway down the fire road you find you cannot remember the sound of your mother's voice, and you understand you made a trade without being told the price. |
| **ANSWER** | Go to the oldest tree in the basin and say your name. | Eleven voices say it back in unison and get it right on the first attempt, which they have never managed before. In the spring a survey team recovers forty-one seconds of speech from a core sample. |
| **WALK OUT** | Service what you can reach, run no reels, say nothing, leave at dawn. | It does not have you. That is the whole of your achievement and it is worth more than it sounds. |

## What the game is careful not to do

- **No monster reveal.** The Chorus is never confirmed, the watcher removes
  itself when looked at directly, and nothing ever explains itself.
- **No possessed protagonist.** Aldaugh's sanity is a measure of how much of
  the basin they have taken in, not a slide toward becoming something.
- **No haunted-forest folklore.** There is no curse, no ritual to break, no
  ancient evil. There is a physical mechanism, a company that covered up an
  industrial accident, and eleven men who did something well-meant and awful
  with a chisel.
- **No jump-scare economy.** Exactly one loud event exists, gated behind high
  sustained tension and a setting the player can turn off without losing
  anything else.
- **Reading is rewarded mechanically.** Documents raise the floor sanity cannot
  fall below. Understanding the basin makes it easier to survive here — and
  harder to leave.
