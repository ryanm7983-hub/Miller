Three forges, one art direction

Everything a small team has to make, briefed properly

Each builder asks the questions a good brief needs, then assembles a prompt far longer and more specific than anyone types by hand.

Art assets

Characters, enemies, platforms, props, backgrounds, foregrounds, UI, VFX, weapons and full biome tilesets. Six art styles from 8-bit pixel to scratchy ink horror, with perspective and negative prompts handled for you.

Music briefs

Describe the track — purpose, mood, instrumentation, tempo, key — and get a prompt formatted for Suno, Udio, Stable Audio, MusicGen, or as a plain brief for a human composer. Prompts only; no audio is generated here.

Code prompts

Pick your engine, genre and the systems you need — from jump feel to save/load to behaviour trees — and get a structured brief with conventions, constraints and output format ready for any coding assistant.

Theme Lock

Set your world, palette and reference once. Every prompt from every builder inherits it, along with an explicit consistency instruction. This is the difference between a folder of sprites and a game.

Your library

Save any prompt, search it later, copy it back out or export the lot to a file. Stored in your browser — no account, no sync, no server.

Genuinely offline

No CDN, no fonts, no analytics, no API keys, no telemetry. Open the file from a USB stick on a plane and every feature still works exactly the same.

How it works

Four steps, then paste

Lock your look

Pick a theme — gothic castle, sunken kingdom, neon dystopia — or write your own world, palette and reference.

Pick what you need

Choose a builder and a preset, then adjust the details. Every field maps to something the model actually responds to.

Generate

PixelForge assembles the positive prompt, the negative prompt and — for animations and tilesets — a whole batch at once.

Paste anywhere

Copy into your image, music or coding tool of choice. Save it to your library so the next asset matches this one.
